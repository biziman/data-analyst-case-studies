# CTV geo-uplift: simulated research case

## Decision

Should a U.S. mobile-app company increase smart-TV (connected TV, or CTV) ad spend? The company wants to know whether CTV causes additional app installs and revenue, and whether the incremental revenue justifies the spend.

**Short answer from this synthetic test:** The treatment markets show a positive estimated lift in installs and revenue. But estimated incremental return on ad spend (iROAS) is only **0.23** (95% interval: **0.16–0.30**). Under a revenue-only decision rule, the result does not support scaling spend for direct revenue payback. This is an illustrative result, not a finding from a real company or prior project.

## Business and measurement context

The product is an Android/iOS app. The proposed treatment is CTV advertising in selected U.S. markets. A key challenge is that iOS attribution signals, including SKAdNetwork (SKAN), are privacy-preserving and aggregated; they are not a causal estimate of what the ads added. Amplitude event data can describe app outcomes, but attributed conversions are not themselves proof of incrementality.

The case therefore uses aggregate app installs and revenue by market and week as the primary outcomes, and a randomized geographic holdout to estimate causal lift. The same geography-level method can include iOS and Android without relying on person-level ad-to-install matching. A real study would first verify that the app analytics and revenue systems can provide consistent, reliable geography-level outcomes.

## Data provenance and base situation

All data are fictional and generated by `generate_data.py` with a fixed seed. The dataset has **1,120 rows**: 40 fictional DMAs × 14 weeks × 2 platforms. It contains 8 pre-campaign weeks and 6 test weeks. Markets are matched into 20 pairs by approximate size; one market in each pair is randomly assigned CTV. The file has one row per market, week, and platform.

`data/geo_weekly.csv` includes installs, revenue, assigned treatment, and campaign spend. There are no user-level identifiers, attributed installs, or actual Amplitude/SKAN exports. The data-generating process includes market size differences, time trends, shared weekly shocks, and random noise. The treatment effect is deliberately not stored in the dataset. The analyst script reads only the dataset and does not use the generator's assumptions.

## Metrics and analysis plan

- **Primary outcomes:** weekly installs and app revenue, summed across iOS and Android at the market level.
- **Treatment effect:** intention-to-treat difference in the pre-to-test change for each matched treatment/control pair. Each market receives equal weight.
- **Uncertainty:** 95% paired t intervals and an exact within-pair randomization test (all 2^20 treatment reallocations).
- **Efficiency:** campaign spend divided by incremental installs (cost per incremental install); incremental revenue divided by spend (iROAS).
- **Decision rule:** scale for direct revenue return only if the lower 95% confidence bound for iROAS is above 1. This is a case-study rule, not a universal business threshold.

The interval for relative lift scales the absolute treatment estimate and interval by the average pre-period control outcome. It is a simple descriptive normalization, not a separately modeled ratio interval. Revenue iROAS intervals use the estimated incremental revenue interval divided by observed test spend.

## Analyst findings

| Outcome | Estimated lift | 95% interval | Estimated total incremental outcome during test |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Installs (both platforms) | +9.4% | +7.1% to +11.7% | 24,143 installs (18,217 to 30,069) |
| Revenue (both platforms) | +12.1% | +8.4% to +15.8% | $136,323 ($94,306 to $178,341) |

Test spend was **$586,094**. Estimated cost per incremental install was **$24.28**. Estimated iROAS was **0.23**, with a 95% interval of **0.16 to 0.30**. In this simulated example, incremental revenue is below the campaign cost across the interval. The test provides evidence of positive incremental outcomes, but those outcomes do not meet the stated direct-revenue payback rule.

Platform breakdowns are secondary and exploratory. The estimated iOS install lift is +9.0% (95% interval +6.9% to +11.1%); Android is +9.9% (+7.2% to +12.5%). Revenue lifts are +9.2% for iOS and +15.7% for Android. These comparisons do not establish that CTV performs differently by platform; the study was not powered for that question.

## Recommendation and limitations

Do not recommend scaling this campaign for direct revenue payback based on this simulated result. If the business values longer-term revenue, subscriptions, or cross-device brand effects, define those outcomes and their observation windows, then run a properly powered test. A real test should validate market-level outcome coverage, keep other marketing and promotions balanced, prevent campaign spillover into controls, lock assignments, and allow enough time for delayed revenue to mature.

This case has only 20 randomized pairs, and estimates use equal market weights. Synthetic data are cleaner than real campaign data; there may be geo contamination, outcome delays, missing data, or uneven delivery. The results do not show that Amplitude or SKAN was used in a real prior project. No conclusion about an actual company's CTV efficiency is supported.

## Reproduce

Python 3.10+ with the packages in `requirements.txt`:

```bash
python -m pip install -r 9-ctv-geo-uplift/requirements.txt
python 9-ctv-geo-uplift/generate_data.py
python 9-ctv-geo-uplift/analyze.py
```

The analysis writes `outputs/data_checks.json`, `outputs/platform_results.csv`, and `outputs/primary_results.csv`.

## Research basis

- [Google Research: Measuring Ad Effectiveness Using Geo Experiments](https://research.google/pubs/measuring-ad-effectiveness-using-geo-experiments/)
- [Google Meridian: Implementation of geo testing](https://developers.google.com/meridian/geox/implementation-of-geo-testing)
- [Meta GeoLift methodology](https://facebookincubator.github.io/GeoLift/docs/Methodology/)
- [Apple: App Store ad attribution](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/ad-attribution/)

The design follows common geo-experiment practices: define the outcome and markets before launch, assess pre-period comparability and power, randomize where feasible, isolate the holdout, and report uncertainty. Geo tests estimate incrementality; attribution systems answer a different question.
